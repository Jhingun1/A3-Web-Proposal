# Presentation Transcript — "Harbourline: Planning Sydney by Experience"
Target length ≈ 4:30 (~660 words at a natural speaking pace). Slides marked [S#].

[S1 — Title]
Hi, I'm Nuo Yan Li. My proposal is **Harbourline** — a Sydney tourism site that plans your visit by *activity, time of day and budget* — not by where things sit on a map.

[S3 — Background research]
The research frames the pitch. Sydney draws about **63.6 million domestic visitors**, spending **$20.8 billion** a year (Destination NSW). International arrivals hit a record **8.9 million**, driving **$192 billion** in total visitor spend (TRA, 2025). And about **64% of international visitors are repeaters** — they've seen the Opera House before and want what's next. So a strong site must move people from the *iconic first visit* to *deeper, return-visit experiences*.

[S4–5 — Gap analysis]
I benchmarked the current leader, sydney.com. Three gaps stand out. Content is organised **by suburb**, so "what can I *do* this free afternoon?" means browsing geography. There's **no time-aware planning** — nothing answers "I have four hours and it's getting dark." And **free versus ticketed is buried in article text**, with no way to build an itinerary. My personas confirm this is exactly where planning breaks down.

[S7–8 — Personas]
So I built three evidence-based personas. **Jenna**, 27 — a first-timer planning two nights on her phone, costs visible the whole way. **David**, 41 — on his fourth visit; he's seen the icons and wants hidden beaches and local food. **Aisha**, 34 — a local planning a *free, walkable* family day, no car. Their shared trait: **time-poor, cost-aware, mobile-first planners who decide fast**. That single insight drove the concept — organise by **experiences and constraints**, not geography.

[S9–10 — Sitemap / IA]
That shaped the information architecture. The sitemap stays shallow: **Experiences, My Plan, Events, Go Deeper, About**. "Go Deeper" is a curated layer for return visitors — that's David. Geography is deliberately **not** a top branch; it's a *facet*, alongside cost, duration and time of day. "My Plan" is promoted to primary because it's the payoff — the moment planning becomes a plan.

[S11–12 — Moodboard & design system]
The visual direction comes from my annotated moodboard. The palette is **"golden-hour harbour"** — harbour ink and blue for structure, sand neutrals for calm, and a sunrise coral used sparingly, under ten percent of the screen, as the single accent for committed actions. Typography pairs **Fraunces** — a serif with a guidebook, heritage feel — with **Inter** for practical UI. The annotation explains how each colour, font and pattern is used.

[S13 — Sketches: breadth]
For the concept I sketched three homepage directions. Version one mirrored the competition — pick a suburb. I discarded it: my personas don't think in postcodes. Version two flipped to **experience-first** with a filter system — but the big hero pushed the filters below the fold on a phone, exactly where my personas plan.

[S14 — Pivotal decision]
So the final concept applies one hierarchy rule: **controls and results before persuasion**. The hero is demoted to a slim banner; chips like *Free*, *under two hours*, *sunset* sit first. The pivotal decision: every card carries **"+ Add to plan"**, backed by a persistent plan strip that shows your stops as you browse. That was driven by Jenna — on her phone, mid-decision, she must be able to *commit without leaving the screen*. On mobile it becomes a sticky bar above the bottom nav, so the plan is always one tap away.

[S15–16 — Wireframes: breadth]
For breadth, the full board has **annotated wireframes for desktop and mobile** — home, the filtered listing, and the itinerary. Key patterns: faceted filtering with a bottom sheet on mobile; list rows that become touch-friendly cards; an itinerary that reorders by drag and live-recomputes cost and distance; and sharing as a **login-free** primary action — the word-of-mouth loop for planning a trip with friends.

[S18–19 — Self-evaluation & closing]
To close: Harbourline **plans by how you want to spend your time, not by where you're standing**. Every artefact — the experience-first IA, the cost and time facets, the persistent plan, the mobile-first layout — traces back to the research and the three personas. That's my proposal. Thank you — happy to take questions.
