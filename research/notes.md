# A3 Web Proposal — Research Notes
**Concept pitch:** *Harbourline* — a Sydney tourism website that plans your visit by how you want to experience the city (activity, time of day, budget), not just where it is.

## 1. Key insights about Sydney (relevant to the pitch)

| Insight | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Sydney is a massive **domestic** destination: 63.6 million domestic visitors in the year ended March 2026, who spent $20.8 billion | Destination NSW, Sydney statistics |
| International tourism is booming nationally: 8.9 million international arrivals in 2025; record $39.2B international visitor spend; total visitor spend $192.4B (+6.5% on 2024) | Tourism Research Australia, Annual Benchmark Report 2025 |
| The icons anchor first visits: Sydney Opera House (10.9M visitors/yr, UNESCO World Heritage) and Sydney Harbour Bridge (8.2M/yr, free pedestrian access, ticketed climbs) | Wikipedia: Tourism in Sydney; Destination NSW |
| The harbour ferry (Manly) is a signature low-cost experience — 20–30 min, front-row views of both icons | Destination NSW |
| Tourism is a key economic driver — the sector is forecast to reach a record ~$315B in 2025 | WTTC |

**Why these matter to the pitch:** the destination's story is "one city, many experiences — waterfront, heritage, food, outdoors, culture." A website must let users move quickly from *iconic first-timer* to *return-visitor depth*. That supports an experience-first (not destination-first) information architecture.

## 2. Competitive / gap analysis of the current tourism web presence (sydney.com)

1. **Content is organised by destination (suburbs)** — users thinking "what can I *do* on a free day?" have to browse geography. → *Gap: activity-first navigation.*
2. **No time-aware planning** — sunrise bridge climb vs. twilight, ferry timing, "I have 4 hours in the CBD." → *Gap: planning by duration/time-of-day.*
3. **Free vs. ticketed is buried in article text** — "Good to know" bullets. → *Gap: scannable cost filter (Free / Ticketed / Budget).*
4. **No multi-day itinerary building** — visitors stitch their own trip by hand. → *Gap: itinerary builder.*
5. **Mobile is the planning device** — trip planning happens on phones, between decisions. → *Gap: mobile-first planning flows (saved lists, quick add-to-day).*

## 3. Audience

- Primary: domestic short-break travellers (NSW/SE Australia) — the 63.6M figure; they visit often, compare options quickly, care about cost and time.
- Secondary: international leisure visitors (8.9M arrivals nationally; 64% are returning visitors per TRA — they want *beyond-the-icons* experiences).
- Tertiary: local residents re-exploring (events, free days out).

## 4. How research shaped design decisions

- Experience-first IA (Experiences as a top-level section, not under "Places") ← gaps 1 & 2.
- Cost + duration + time-of-day filters on every listing ← gaps 2 & 3.
- Itinerary builder with save-to-day + share ← gap 4.
- Mobile-first wireframes; bottom-tab nav; sticky "add to plan" ← gap 5.
- Persona "David (returning international visitor)" explicitly asks for beyond-the-icons → curated "Go deeper" collections.
- Persona "Jenna (domestic first-timer, 2-day budget trip)" → cost filters and a "free things" quick path.
