A1What I was testingThe “obvious” mirror of sydney.com: geography as the primary axis. Safe, familiar IA.
A2Why it failed the briefMy personas plan by activity and time, not postcode. Jenna (“free 4-hour day in the CBD”) and Aisha (“free, this weekend, no car”) would have to click 3–4 suburbs to answer one question. It also hides the free/ticketed distinction inside each suburb article.
A3OutcomeDropped the geographic nav as the primary axis — kept geographic grouping as a secondary filter on the listing page (see V3), so power users can still browse by place.

Iteration note: after the first test with persona cards, I moved the filter bar up — but the hero still pushes results below the fold on mobile.
B1What changed vs V1Primary axis flipped to experience categories; nav shrunk to 4 items; filters introduced (activity/time/budget/area) — the core “decide fast” idea.
B2Problem found in sketch reviewThe hero + headline consume the first viewport, so filters and results sit below the fold on a phone — exactly where my personas are planning (Jenna, mobile-first). A 2-day-trip planner should see options and controls immediately, not a slogan.
B3Decision carried into V3Compress the hero to a slim banner, move filters above the grid, and add a persistent “plan” strip. Hierarchy rule: controls & results before persuasion.
