1Nav: IA at a glance4 items max — Experiences (core), Events, Go deeper (curated, for returning visitors), About. Geography is a filter, not a nav item: the persona research showed users think in activities, not postcodes.
2“My plan (2)”The itinerary counter is always visible — visible progress is the core value of the concept. Coral dot = live count; clicking opens the plan page/drawer.
3Hero demotedOne line of promise, no full-bleed persuasion (decision from sketch V2→V3: controls & results before persuasion). Image carries mood; text sits on the dark lower band for ≥4.5:1 contrast.
4Inline searchFree-text planning query (“free, under 2h, evening”) — maps to the same filter state, so search and chips stay in sync. Primary CTA in coral: the only hot element in the viewport.
5Filter chips = the productCost (Free) and time (<2h) are the first two chips because 2 of 3 personas are cost/time-constrained. Multi-select; “Area” chip expands to geographic browse (keeps V1 power for place-browsers).
6Result cardsFixed 4:1 media, name, one meta line (duration · area · cost), single action. Free/ticketed badge carries TEXT, not colour alone (accessibility, colour-blind safe). Uniform card size = scannable comparison.
7“+ Add to plan”Consistent, low-cost commitment action on every card — the bridge from discovery to itinerary. Toggled state (✓ Added) gives instant feedback; 44px min height for touch on mobile.
8Plan strip (persistent)Shows current day’s stops as chips so users never lose their progress while browsing. Dashed border = “your content, in flux”. Collapses to a bottom bar on mobile.

9Refine chips stay in syncSame chip system as home — state is shared, so the filter language never changes page to page. Active chips render inverted (ink fill) = unambiguous selected state.
10Faceted sidebarDuration (range), time-of-day, area, access. “Best at sunset” style time awareness answers the #1 research gap: current sites are not time-aware. Sidebar hidden → slide-over on mobile (bottom sheet on touch).
11Result rows, not cardsOn a listing page users compare many items; a row with one meta line per item scans faster than a card grid. Meta line order = decision order: cost → duration → best time → location → one detail.
12Two actions, clear priorityPrimary “+ Add to plan” (coral) vs. secondary “Details” (ghost). No third CTA — every item should be addable, so the plan is the goal, not the article.
13Infinite scroll + “load older” fallback14 items render in ~3 rows on desktop; results paginate by scroll to keep momentum, with a keyboard-accessible “Load more” button (scroll-only traps keyboard users).

14Day tabsMulti-day trips are the return-visitor case (David: 10 nights). Dashed “Add day” keeps extension obvious. Active day = ink fill; the rest of the page re-renders per day.
15Day summary panelAuto-computed: time, cost, distance. This is the payoff for the cost/time research — users see the trip’s shape, not just a list. The “tip” line is where weather-aware suggestions land (research gap: daily re-planning).
16Drag to reorderGrab handles on every row; reordering re-computes times, walking legs and the summary panel instantly. Reordering is the single biggest “planning” interaction on the site.
17Edit / remove inlineEach stop is editable (change time, note) or removable without leaving the page — low-friction iteration, matching “decide fast” persona behaviour.
18Share = the retention hookJenna’s case: send the day to her friend. One link, no login, phone-optimised; PDF export for print. Share is promoted to a primary action because it’s the concept’s word-of-mouth loop.
